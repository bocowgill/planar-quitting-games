# Uniform Nash payoff in quitting games with planar singleton rewards

## Theorem

In an ordinary finite quitting game each player simultaneously chooses Continue or Quit. The first nonempty quitting coalition ends the game with its specified vector. All-continue gives an arbitrary fixed live-stage payoff. Suppose only that the payoff vectors of the singleton coalitions have affine dimension at most two. Then the game has a uniform Nash equilibrium payoff as defined in FC #7957. The number of players, rewards of all larger coalitions, and payoff from perpetual play are unrestricted. Absorbing prizes may be paid starting on the next stage. This result concerns a subclass of the full conjecture.

Write the singleton vectors as columns $`r^j_i=b_i+a_i\cdot R_j`$, with $`a_i,R_j\in\mathbb R^2`$. Let $`s_i=r^i_i`$ and $`A_{ij}=r_i^j-s_i=a_i\cdot(R_j-R_i)`$. Denote the reward from coalition $`S\ne\varnothing`$ by $`r(S)`$. We first work in general position: normals are nonzero and pairwise nonparallel, no three of the lines $`\ell_i(z)=a_i\cdot(z-R_i)=0`$ concur, and $`R_i`$ lies on no other line. The inequalities below use only this finite list of conditions. Boundary cases follow by perturbation at the end.

## Stage comparisons on the full cube

Let $`p_i\in[0,1]`$ be a player's stationary hazard. Conditional on this date being reached draw rivals independently. Let $`h_i`$ be the probability of a nonempty set of rival quitters, $`C_i`$ the expected payoff from that event times its indicator when $`i`$ continues, and $`Q_i`$ the expected payoff when $`i`$ quits immediately. Define $`F_i=C_i-h_i Q_i`$. Near zero,

```math
 F_i(p)=\sum_{j\ne i} A_{ij}p_j+O\big((\sum_{j\ne i}p_j)^2\big).
```

These are polynomials in rival hazards. Nonnegative $`F_i`$ means that waiting until a rival stops yields at least the value of quitting, when $`h_i>0`$. When at least two players have positive hazards, the exact stationary equilibrium conditions are $`F_i\ge0`$ at $`p_i=0`$, $`F_i=0`$ at $`0<p_i<1`$, and $`F_i\le0`$ at $`p_i=1`$. Against stationary rivals every planned quit date interpolates between $`Q_i`$ and $`C_i/h_i`$. Alternatively their scalar Bellman inequalities can be iterated since the rivals stop in bounded expected time. Thus the comparisons cover every unilateral behavior, and replacing terminal payoffs by physical averages costs $`O(1/N)`$ uniformly over deviations. Honest payoffs also converge. The hidden independent dates represent all behaviors before absorption since there is a single live history at each date.

Put $`U=\{i:A_{ij}>0\text{ for every }j\ne i\}`$ and $`K=I\setminus U`$. Each $`k\in K`$ has a rival $`j`$ whose solo reward to $`k`$ is at most $`s_k`$. This rival can cap $`k`$ by attempting Quit with a small fixed hazard $`\eta`$ while all others continue. A deviator meets the rival on its first quit with probability at most $`\eta`$, regardless of its behavioral strategy. On either singleton the expected payoff is at most $`s_k`$. The rival's geometric duration gives a further $`O(1/(\eta N))`$ cost in $`N`$-stage averages. Signs of physical coverage can therefore be checked without knowing any minmax value.

If $`K`$ is empty and every $`c_i\ge s_i`$ in the live payoff $`c`$, everyone can wait. Otherwise choose an actor with $`c_i<s_i`$ to quit with small constant hazard. That actor cannot improve by waiting against rivals who never stop. Every rival weakly prefers the volunteer's prize to their own solo reward and can tie it with probability at most the hazard. A geometric tail controls the horizon approximation for those rivals. For the volunteer any horizon payoff is bounded above by $`s_i+O(1/N)`$, including when that player prevents absorption. This settles this case, including a single player.

To handle $`K\ne\varnothing`$, modify each row $`u\in U`$ to $`\widetilde F_u=F_u+\chi_u(p_{-u})p_u`$, leaving other rows unchanged. Choose continuous $`\chi_u\ge0`$ equal to one near zero and supported on a neighborhood where $`F_u\ge0`$. This is possible by the expansion and strict positivity of its coefficients, including $`F_u=0`$ when all rivals wait. Whenever the added term is positive, $`\widetilde F_u>0`$, which rules out a cube-complementarity solution there since $`p_u>0`$. Thus every solution of the modified comparisons also satisfies the original comparisons. A solution with exactly one positive hazard cannot have its sole quitter in $`U`$.

If only $`k\in K`$ quits with some probability $`p>0`$ at such a solution, all rivals' comparisons hold at that actual $`p`$, also when $`p=1`$. Use the prescription through a finite date $`H`$, switching on live survival to the explicit punishment of $`k`$ above. Other players can reach the changed tail with probability at most $`(1-p)^H`$, since $`k`$'s trials continue whatever they do before absorption. For $`k`$, a pre-cutoff absorption necessarily yields $`s_k`$, and the punishment caps its remaining-horizon payoff at $`s_k+O(\eta)+O(1/(\eta(N-H)))`$. Honest payoff approaches $`r^k`$ within $`O((1-p)^H+H/N)`$. Choose $`\eta,H`$ and then the horizon threshold. This handles a behavioral deviation which suppresses every prescribed quit.

## Degree at unanimous continuation

Consider $`P=\{z:\ell_i(z)\ge0,\ i\in K\}`$. If some $`R_k`$ for $`k\in K`$ belongs to $`P`$, a single volunteer $`k`$ with vanishingly small hazard gives a solution. Every other member of $`K`$ receives at least their solo reward. Members of $`U`$ receive strictly more, since $`k\ne u`$. A rival's improvement from trying to tie is bounded by a constant times the hazard and is incurred only at its first attempt. Apply the deterministic cutoff and punishment of $`k`$ just described. We henceforth assume $`R_k\notin P`$ for every $`k\in K`$.

For a matrix $`B`$ the homogeneous normal map is $`n_B(x)=Bx^++x-x^+`$, with componentwise positive part. Its zeros encode $`t=x^+\ge0,\ Bt\ge0,\ t_i(Bt)_i=0`$. Put $`B=A_{K,K}`$. A nonzero homogeneous solution would give $`z=\sum t_iR_i/\sum t_i\in P`$, with each participating constraint an equality. At most two lines contain $`z`$. A single participant would have $`z=R_i\in P`$. Two participants on different, nonparallel lines cannot average to their intersection: applying one constraint to the weighted average gives a nonzero contribution from the other participant. Thus there is only the zero root. Compactness of the unit sphere and positive homogeneity give $`\|n_B(x)\|\ge c\|x\|`$ for some $`c>0`$. Its degree $`d`$ may be calculated at any regular target on a sufficiently large ball.

The derivative of $`\widetilde F`$ at zero is $`D=A+\operatorname{diag}({\bf1}_U)`$. Any homogeneous solution has zero on $`U`$ because those rows of $`D`$ have strictly positive entries. The remaining problem has matrix $`B`$, hence $`n_D`$ also has only the zero root. Its degree equals $`d`$: choose any regular core offset $`q_K`$ for the calculation below and positive $`q_U`$. In the problem $`q+Dt\ge0,\ t\perp(q+Dt)`$ all $`U`$-coordinates vanish and have strict slack. The regular preimages and the signs of the active principal minors are therefore exactly those of the core. Equivalently one may homotope the upper block to the positive identity, with core rows decoupled after setting $`t_U=0`$.

Write $`\pi(x)`$ for coordinatewise projection onto the unit cube. The map $`G(x)=x-\pi(x)+\widetilde F(\pi(x))`$ is the identity plus a bounded continuous map; its global degree is $`+1`$ by straight-line homotopy on a large ball. Its zeros give exactly the solutions of the modified cube comparisons. Near zero it is $`n_D(x)+O(\|x\|^2)`$, so its local degree there is $`d`$ by the sphere bound. If $`d\ne1`$, additivity of Brouwer degree forces another zero. Its nonzero hazard profile solves the original game by the preceding section. A zero of $`G`$ with $`\pi(x)=0`$ has $`x=0`$, since $`\widetilde F(0)=0`$.

## What the planar geometry adds

For a fixed $`z_0`$ use the offset $`q_i=\ell_i(z_0)`$ on the core. With $`t_i\ge0`$ set

```math
 Z=\frac{z_0+\sum_{i\in K}t_i R_i}{1+\sum_{i\in K}t_i}.
 \qquad q_i+(Bt)_i=(1+\sum_j t_j)\ell_i(Z).
```

Thus any solution of the offset complementarity problem gives $`Z\in P`$, lying on the boundary line of each positive variable. If $`P`$ is empty there is no preimage at this target, hence $`d=0`$. If nonempty it has nonempty interior under the stated general-position assumptions: at a boundary point at most two independent inequalities are tight, and one can move strictly inward for both. Choose $`z_0`$ in its interior. Zero weights then give a strictly complementary solution of local sign $`+1`$. A singleton positive weight is impossible because its own constraint has $`q_i>0`$ and $`B_{ii}=0`$.

A solution with positive support $`i,j`$ must give their polygon vertex $`Z`$. Its equations are $`q_i+A_{ij}t_j=0`$ and $`q_j+A_{ji}t_i=0`$, so it exists with both entries positive exactly when $`A_{ij}<0`$ and $`A_{ji}<0`$ there. When these signs hold, the unique weights produce the intersection of the two lines by the displayed identity, so all other inequalities have strict slack. The active determinant is $`-A_{ij}A_{ji}<0`$, giving local sign $`-1`$. The degree is the sum of these signs, because all solutions at this offset are strictly complementary and their normal maps are nonsingular on the respective orthants.

Each nonredundant edge of $`P`$ lies on the line of a player $`i`$ and is oriented along that edge toward $`R_i`$. The point lies outside the edge by assumption, so the direction is unambiguous. At a vertex shared by $`i,j`$, the edge of $`j`$ points toward that vertex precisely when $`A_{ij}<0`$: its point $`R_j`$ is then on the forbidden side of line $`i`$. Interchanging the indices gives the other sign. Call a vertex with two incoming edges a sink. The computation gives $`d=1-\#\{\text{sinks}\}`$. Strictly redundant constraints do not occur in any active support.

An unbounded full-dimensional polygon with a vertex has its boundary edges in a chain between two rays under these general-position assumptions. Both rays point toward their finite endpoints, since each owner's point is outside its feasible ray. The chain therefore has a sink. A boundary that is an entire line already contains its owner's point; a strip requires parallel normals. The degree argument consequently handles each unbounded possibility that reaches this step.

For bounded $`P`$, the only remaining case is a polygon with no sink. Then each vertex has one incoming and one outgoing edge and the entire perimeter is a directed cycle. On a directed edge from $`z`$ to $`z'`$ owned by $`j`$, both points and $`R_j`$ are collinear, with $`z'`$ strictly between $`z`$ and $`R_j`$. Let $`D_j=\log(\|R_j-z\|/\|R_j-z'\|)>0`$. In backwards order the flow $`dv/du=R_j-v`$ travels from $`z`$ to $`z'`$ in time $`D_j`$. Split it into $`M`$ equal substeps. The backwards update in a substep is exactly $`v^+=(1-p)v+pR_j`$ with $`p=1-\exp(-D_j/M)`$.

Run these substeps in reverse chronological order, periodically, with the edge owner alone attempting quit with probability $`p`$. All randomization is by this player's ordinary independent behavioral move. Honest absorption is geometric in the number of periods. The specified vertex and subdivision values, evaluated by $`b_i+a_i\cdot v`$, solve the honest continuation equations; uniqueness by geometric absorption identifies them with the genuine expected payoffs, also for $`U`$. In particular the values for $`U`$ are mixtures of the payoffs from single quitters in $`K`$, all above their solo levels. For $`K`$ each target lies in $`P`$. At its own substeps the volunteer is indifferent: the pre- and post-values on its own edge and the singleton all pay $`s_i`$. At other substeps continuing has the proposed value. Quitting can beat that value by at most $`R\max_j(1-e^{-D_j/M})`$, where $`R`$ bounds payoff ranges, since the deviation receives its solo payoff except for a simultaneous volunteer quit. That deviation terminates play, so the error is charged only once, including after any history of skipping one's own attempts. Every player faces at least one other volunteer with strictly positive aggregate hazard in each full period. Thus the expected duration before absorption is bounded uniformly over all unilateral deviations at each fixed $`M`$. Iteration proves the terminal bounds. Physical averages differ by $`O_M(1/N)`$ for every deviation. Starting at the same chosen vertex for each $`M`$ gives a single target and taking $`M`$ large before the horizon threshold yields the FC quantifiers.

## Degeneracies and scope

Every affine-plane representation can be approximated by general-position $`(a_i,R_i)`$: the exceptions are finitely many proper polynomial zero sets in their unconstrained real coordinates. For example normals, incidences and triple intersections can be broken by arbitrarily small independent choices of lines and then generic points on them. Vectors of dimension zero or one have a representation on the same two-dimensional domain. Keep every payoff outside the singleton columns fixed. If the perturbation changes each singleton coordinate by at most $`\xi`$, every behavioral profile's expected average payoff differs by at most $`\xi`$, at every horizon. Its regret differs by at most $`2\xi`$. A convergent subsequence of equilibrium targets of the approximating games therefore gives a single target of the original game. For each tolerance one fixed approximate game's strategy works simultaneously for all horizons beyond its threshold. From an absorbing initial state use its true permanent payoff and arbitrary behavior.

The proof uses ordinary finite-dimensional Brouwer degree, including homotopy invariance, additivity and the local determinant sign at a regular linear zero. It also gives the arbitrary-dimensional sufficient degree test for $`D`$, whenever its core homogeneous map has only the zero root. The two-dimensional step is the count of active pairs followed by the directed perimeter construction. In three dimensions active triples contribute to the index, and no perimeter cycle follows from degree one.

The Lean proof computes the signed count directly using the divergence theorem, change of variables, and smoothing away from the locally affine roots. The no-sink branch follows binding lines and finds a repeated pair of constraints. The formalized perturbation interpolates toward tangent lines of a parabola to avoid finitely many roots of incidence polynomials.
